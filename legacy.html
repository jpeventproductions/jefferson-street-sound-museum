2004 Jefferson Street · Nashville, Tennessee

Side 02 · The Legacy

The street behind the sound.

Before Nashville’s music industry became a global brand, Jefferson Street was already alive with clubs, artists and audiences.

The Original Music Row

A nightly constellation of sound.

From the 1940s into the 1970s, Jefferson Street was an essential destination for Black musicians traveling through the South and a launchpad for Nashville talent. Jazz, blues, soul and rhythm & blues moved between neighborhood rooms and out across the country.

1940s

The rooms come alive

Nightclubs, theaters and gathering places turn Jefferson Street into a center of Black entertainment and commerce.

1950s

A required stop

Touring artists share stages with local musicians, creating a network of influence heard far beyond Nashville.

1960s

Sound and social change

The music district stands beside Fisk, Meharry and Tennessee State during a defining Civil Rights era.

1968

The interstate arrives

Construction of I-40 cuts through North Nashville, displacing businesses and permanently changing the district.

Today

The story keeps spinning

The museum preserves the names, sounds and memories that development could not erase.

Artists in the grooves

Jimi Hendrix, Etta James, Little Richard, Ray Charles, Jackie Shane, Ike & Tina Turner, Marion James, Jimmy Church, Frank Howard and many more are part of the story preserved here.
